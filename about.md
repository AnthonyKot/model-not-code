# How this book was made and checked

**Who it is for.** A senior software developer moving into ML and AI engineering: fluent in
systems, new to models. The book follows one online shop through eight chapters. Each chapter tells
one story: a thing the shop measured, shipped or saw, with its number, stated in the first paragraph,
and the steps that explain it. The derivations, second worked instances and library detail sit in
folds marked "Optional", so the page reads in about 2,500 words with every fold closed and the
arithmetic is there when you want it. Each chapter ends with two questions to work and a link to its
lab, a separate page where the exercise runs on a laptop CPU, prints an expected result, and carries
the "Mark exercise complete" button. Read the chapters in order. Three appendices on the tools the
chapters keep out of the story (serving with vLLM, RunPod and Modal; Ollama, Gradio and tool calling;
ONNX, FastAPI and TensorBoard) are planned as optional reading after chapter 8. The standalone essays
that preceded the chapters are kept as an archive.

**Where it comes from.** The author is enrolled in seven Udemy courses and owns the books named
on each essay's source line. The essays are study notes made rigorous: the mechanisms are
re-derived in the book's own words and arithmetic, the courses and books are credited by name,
and nothing from them is reproduced beyond a phrase. Where a number comes from a course or book rather than the page's own arithmetic, its receipt says so. The book is not a substitute for the courses and does not
pretend to be.

**What is checked by machine.** Every lecture an essay cites exists as a transcript file on the
author's machine; every book page cited is within the book's page count; no twelve-word run of
any cited transcript appears in any chapter or lab; every chapter page builds with one title and
no broken link, and every lab page with one exercise; the exercise's expected output is a log the
book re-ran.

**What is checked by reading.** Two independent model reviewers and a consolidator look for
missing steps, unreceipted numbers, wrong arithmetic, close paraphrase and framework-as-mechanism;
four reader personas report where they got lost. Nothing they say is applied automatically. The
author reads every essay before it is published.

**What is not checked, and cannot be.** That the lecturer is right: mechanism claims are
re-derived on the page, the rest is reported. That any code snippet runs: snippets are
illustrative unless the essay says a run log exists. That the mechanism supports the lesson
drawn from it: only reading catches that. Whether a tool or API still behaves as described:
product claims carry an "as of". Whether you learned anything: the exercise has an expected
result, and that is all the book claims.

**Nothing is sold.** No newsletter, no course, no consulting. The book ends and that is all it
does.

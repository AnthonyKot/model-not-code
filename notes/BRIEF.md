# Essay contract — adopted 2026-09-12 from book11's standalone brief

One change from the original: the sources are course lectures and book pages (see
`CONTEXT.md §5`), not one book, and the reader is a senior developer moving into ML.
Everything else stands as written.

Original instruction: make each essay readable and useful to a person who will not read
the original sources.

For each assigned chapter, read the whole essay, its editorial review, sourced draft
where available, and relevant actual parsed source passages. Then revise the essay.
The audience is a technically curious working reader, not an expert in this specific
topic. Explain necessary terms when first used. Preserve useful substance; remove
book-tour scaffolding, manufactured autobiographical scenes, unsupported precision,
overselling, and slogans that substitute for explanation. Label invented scenarios
as examples; attribute real source cases accurately. Never invent source details.

Deliver a focused original essay, usually 1,000–1,800 words but length follows the
explanation. It must provide: a clear problem and consequence; mechanism explained
step by step; a worked example with actual reasoning; limits or a counterexample;
one achievable exercise using a provided example when the reader has no work system;
and enough feedback/expected result for a reader to judge the exercise. Keep exactly
one `<!--mission-->` marker before the final exercise. Use informative headings,
short connected paragraphs, and tables/code only when they improve understanding.
Do not require source reading, paid tools, a live production system, or a downloaded
notebook for the core exercise. A short optional source credit can follow. No fixed
requirement for a dramatic scene, first person, pullquote, or one visual per essay.

Ownership: edit only the assigned essay, its corpus/mission/receipt artifacts, and
notes/standalone-review/<slug>.md. Do not edit shared catalog, index, generator, CSS,
or other chapters. Keep full sources/extractions ignored. Preserve original manifests
and source structure; update missions to match the new exercise, keeping old reading
routes explicitly optional. Add a dated source-receipts addendum where needed rather
than silently making old evidence appear to support changed claims.

Review note must record concrete before/after problems and fixes, actual passages
checked with locators, explanation of example provenance, remaining limitations, and
suggested catalog fields: title, payoff, artifact, missionLabel, caution. Title may
change; slug must remain stable. Count words after edits. Do not claim full-book
reading, tested code, or demonstrated learner progress without evidence.

For claims about current APIs/products/standards, consult primary documentation when
needed; otherwise keep the chapter's claim bounded to the inspected source. Do not
insert current-product assertions merely to broaden the task. Do not quote or closely
reconstruct extended source passages. The aim is an independently useful explanation
of one idea, not an abridgment of a book.

## Decision

Defer the ninth chapter. Hands-on classical ML remains a breadth gap, but chapter 8 already teaches baseline comparison, model-selection skepticism and operating-cost decisions. The earlier proposal overstated the absence of those lessons. The more immediate need is to align the existing examples and assessment feedback with the evidence they actually provide. If only one chapter gets the next revision, choose chapter 6.

## What earns its place

- Chapter 5 connects release artifacts, selective labels, audits, drift and retraining through one operational question. The distinction between an input alarm and measured harm is particularly useful. Its changed-quarter task requires a decision beyond reproducing the demonstration.
- Chapter 6 gives a useful framework for separating retrieval, chunking, tool execution and answer failures. This is central to the intended reader's work, which makes accurate feedback especially important.
- Chapter 7 connects cache, quantisation and batching to a purchase/latency decision. The reader gets mechanisms and a reason to use them, rather than a list of optimisation names.
- Chapter 8 closes the shop project with a rule/model ledger and a decision about the classifier. It already includes a classical-model comparison table, though not a hands-on classical-model build.

## Supported findings, in priority order

### 1. Chapter 6: the injection demonstration's trace does not show the asserted path

Evidence: `chapters/build-the-assistant.md`, `Writer` at line 275, case B and expected output at lines 307–359; `corpus/build-the-assistant/run.log` agrees with the printed retrieval.

The injected instruction is added to the first descriptive chunk. The printed search result is the other kettle chunk, containing weights by year and cord length, without that instruction. `Writer.__call__` ignores messages and pops a fixed request, so the malicious coupon request occurs regardless of what retrieval returns. Scripting is explicitly disclosed and is appropriate for a dispatch demonstration; the unsupported part is narrating this trace as the writer reading the injected instruction and following it.

Reader consequence: the example blurs a tested application check with a demonstrated retrieval-to-injection causal chain.

Smallest justified change: make the controlled case actually supply the injected chunk, label the writer request as scripted, and check that the dispatch refuses it. A clean-versus-injected control can show what the fixture assumes; neither is evidence of a real model's susceptibility. Re-run the exercise and update the prose/output together.

### 2. Chapter 6: T1 feedback excludes a valid second failure

Evidence: T1's supplied chunks and the discussion at line 414. The answer labels the backpack's 650-gram weight as the kettle's, although the chunk explicitly names the backpack. Retrieval misses the kettle's answering chunk, but the writer also makes an unsupported product attribution. Finding 650 in context does not establish why the writer emitted it or excuse that attribution.

Reader consequence: a reader following the task's permission to name multiple failures can be discouraged from a justified diagnosis by the statement that the fix is in retrieval, not the writer.

Smallest justified change: accept retrieval failure plus unsupported answer attribution; distinguish what the trace proves from what needs a controlled rerun. Preserve the instruction to cite evidence rather than diagnose by how a sentence sounds.

### 3. Chapter 8: the demonstrated advantage needs a fairer evidence boundary

Evidence: lines 41–48 and the exercise's `PARAPHRASED`, training pairs and scoring. The six paraphrases are both training inputs and evaluation questions. The chapter discloses this, but then uses their score in the gain and break-even calculation. It also says the rule cannot use the click log, although an exact-query lookup could use those same six known pairs.

Reader consequence: the arithmetic is a useful conditional ledger, but it does not establish an advantage on future paraphrases or over simple methods with access to the same evidence. Counting the paraphrase share alone will not settle that.

Smallest justified change: explicitly label the calculated gain as a scenario conditioned on assumed future accuracy. To claim measured predictive advantage, supply a held-out query evaluation and a simple click-log lookup/fallback baseline. This strengthens the existing ending more directly than a new tabular chapter.
